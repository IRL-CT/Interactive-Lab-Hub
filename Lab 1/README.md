## Part 0. Know Your Master


The users of Paul Revere’s lanterns were soldiers who used the lightning as means to communicate important information. 
Response warrants how the Americans would respond or prepare for a land attack vs a sea attack
The American soldiers/settlers would be present and their reactions depend on which lantern is lit 
I.e if one lantern is lit soldiers may prepare boots for walking and setting up a land defense compared to if f two then by sea 
The piece is a central component of the American Revolutionary war. 


## Part A. Plan

- **Setting:** Old North Church in 1775

- **Players:** A soldier near the Old North Church who is on guard at night awaiting the signal. Additionally, townspeople are also warned. 

- **Activity:** The soldiers notice the number of lanterns that are present to deduce the next steps they may need to take against the British (prepare for battle or wait).

- **Goals:** Each solider is trying to understand what the code of the lanterns means so awaiting for the next signal to know how to act

## Part C. Prototype the Light (light first!)

We used the Tinkerbelle tool: the phone in the browser was the light, and the laptop drove its brightness and color. Here we had to use two laptops with two phones to drive each phone separately to display one lamp or two lamps. Essentially in each video the person is a US solider awaiting a signal aka a lamp or two to turn on to act accordingly. Since the Tinkerbelle tool operates phone brightness, when a lamp needed to be turned on the phone would reach peak brightness and color and would fade to black otherwise. This apporach interacted with the soliders sight senses.
There were some initial difficulties with the setup for the recording as multiple devices were in use, but it did not take much time for understanding how to overcome that hurdle. One of us recorded and handled the laptops while the other person acted as the US solider waiting for the lamp to go off.

## Remix, Update, or Critique the Master

While majority of the interaction is sight based via the light produced by the phones, to make the lamp affect more clear we decided to address sound interaction by including audio that a laptop can drive on a phone similar to its color and brightness. The file Index.js is the implementation that goes with the Tinkerbelle tool and it allows a fire crackle sound to play on a cell phone to imitate that of fire burning in a lamp. This further emphasizes the lamp interaction that may have occurred in the originial story.
